Ways of working across a global MES rollout

Senior Agile Coach, embedded in the build stream

The problem. A manufacturing execution system rolling out across a multi-site production network. The technology plan was sound; the interfaces between the build streams were where the programme lost time. Decisions that needed two streams to agree had no forum designed to carry them, so they surfaced late and were re-opened often.

What we did. Listened before proposing anything: a structured interview pass across the programme, from the shop floor to leadership, to separate a missing mechanism from a problem that only looked like one. Then a facilitated working session that put the real issues in the order they could actually be solved, and a written diagnosis the sponsors could act on.

Where it landed. A standing way of working the programme runs itself: the recurring formats, each with a named owner and an explicit criterion for handing it over — designed so that the exit does not depend on the coach staying.

Agile product management on a regulatory submission spine

Product Manager (SAFe), across an agile release train

The problem. Chemistry, Manufacturing and Controls is the documentary spine of a drug filing: the part a health authority reads to decide whether a medicine may be made. Digitalising it means running agile delivery in a process where an error is not a bug, it is a regulatory event.

What we did. Owned the product definition against business and epic owners; kept several teams on one solution intent rather than several local optima; weighed every increment as desirable, viable, feasible — and sustainable under audit, which is the constraint that quietly kills most of these programmes.

Where it landed. The programme reached its target scope ahead of the contracted schedule.

One owner for the project and the change around it

Product Owner / Project Manager

The problem. A workflow transformation carrying the split that usually defeats them: project delivery sitting in one role, change and adoption in another, and no single person accountable for the distance between the two.

What we did. Took both. Project management and team coordination, stakeholder engagement, business analysis, change management, user training, and QA and test management under one accountable role.

Where it landed. Still running. The design point is that the decision about the system and the decision about how people will work with it get made by the same person, in the same week.

An operating system for a one-person firm

Builder

The problem. A solo practice has the same operational surface as a small company — pipeline, delivery forecasting, knowledge, publishing, bookkeeping — and none of the staff. Either the back office eats the billable week or it gets automated.

What we did. Built it: a linked knowledge base that is also the CMS for this website, budget-burn forecasting straight off the bookkeeping API, and a set of AI agents that handle the routine departmental work under review. Roughly a working day a week of administration, gone.

Where it landed. Included here deliberately: when we advise on AI adoption and data governance, it is from having run it in production against our own money, not from a vendor deck.
