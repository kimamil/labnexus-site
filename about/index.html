About

Small on purpose

An independent Swiss consultancy for life-science organisations. One partner carries the work from analysis to running change — which caps how much we take on, and is the point.

We work where the science is not the bottleneck. In most of the organisations we are called into, the constraint is not the chemistry or the biology — it is the systems around it, the handovers between teams, and the number of people who have to agree before anything can move.

Regulated is our normal case, not an exception that derails the plan. Manufacturing IT, regulatory submissions, quality systems: places where a system has to be validated, an audit trail has to hold, and "we'll fix it later" is not available as a strategy.

Most of what reaches us today as an AI question — who may see this data, what happens when the model is wrong, how do we prove any of it — is a governance and security question wearing new clothes. We answer it from that side, which is usually the side that decides whether a pilot ever becomes production.

And we build, not only advise. The firm you are reading about runs on software we wrote ourselves: the delivery forecasting, the knowledge graph that is also the CMS behind this site, the automation that keeps the back office small. When we advise on AI adoption, it is from having shipped it into production against our own money.

Why one partner

Because the failure mode of consulting is the handover. A partner sells the thinking, a team you have not met delivers it, and the insight leaks out somewhere in between. LabNexus is built so the person who analysed your problem is the person in the room when it gets solved.

In practice that means a small number of mandates, an honest no when we are not the right answer, and a preference for leaving behind a mechanism your own people run rather than a dependency on us. The selected work shows what that looks like in the field.

The person behind it

Manuel Kammermann

Founder · LabNexus GmbH

Trained as a molecular biologist (MSc), he moved from the bench to the systems around it and has worked inside global pharma in Switzerland since 2018 — as product manager, product owner, project lead and agile coach, on manufacturing IT and regulatory submission programmes.

Before that he ran information security for a software company as its CISO, building out the ISO 27001 side and the security operations around it. That pairing — the lab, then the delivery, then the governance — is why we can sit in a room with scientists, IT and quality and be a peer to each of them rather than a translator between them.
