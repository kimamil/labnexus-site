About

Manuel Kammermann

A molecular biologist who moved from the bench to the systems around it — and stayed for the hard part.

I started in the lab. A master's in molecular biology, pipettes, and the slow discovery that the thing slowing the science down was almost never the science — it was the systems, the handovers, and the number of people who had to agree before anything could move.

So I moved to that side. Since 2018 I have worked inside global pharma in Switzerland — manufacturing IT, regulatory submissions, quality systems — as a product manager, product owner, project lead, and agile coach. The common thread is regulated environments: places where a system has to be validated, an audit trail has to hold, and "we'll fix it later" is not available as a strategy.

Before that I ran information security for a software company as its CISO, building the ISO 27001 side and the security operations around it. That turns out to matter more every year: most of what gets asked today as an AI question — who may see this data, what happens when the model is wrong, how do we prove any of it — is a governance and security question wearing new clothes.

I also build. Not slides about building — the firm you are reading about runs on software I wrote myself: the forecasting, the knowledge graph behind this site, the automation that keeps the back office small. When I advise on AI adoption, it is from the position of having shipped it into production against my own money.

Why LabNexus is one person

Because the failure mode of consulting is the handover. A partner sells the thinking, a team you have not met delivers it, and the insight leaks out somewhere between the two. LabNexus is built so that the person who analysed your problem is the person in the room when it gets solved — which caps how much work I can take, and is the point.

What that means in practice: I take a small number of mandates, I say no when I am not the right answer, and I would rather leave behind a mechanism your own people run than a dependency on me. You can see the shape of that work here.
